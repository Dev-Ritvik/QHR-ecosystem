@@ load-dark-wait | HIGH | systemic | load-slow-line-empty, load-press-wait, load-404-curtain
L: Every page, first visit. Film (/) and inner pages, through the public link, 1920×945.
E: The loading curtain (dark ground, the words "Quality Homes Reality" at about 10 px and 62% strength, a 112 px hairline).
O: A first-time visitor looks at a near-black screen for 12 to 17 seconds before anything of the site is shown. Measured through the link tonight: film 13.5 s, /why-us 11–14 s, /hall 14–17 s. On this machine with no network in the way it is 6.8 s for the film and 3.6–7.2 s for inner pages.
V: Frames at 0.3, 0.8, 1.5, 2.5, 4, 6, 8 and 10 s are identical dark frames (mean brightness 18 of 255); the first picture is in the 12.5 s frame. The link runs over this machine's own line, but the wait is not the link's doing: at a simulated 20 Mbit/s with no link in the way the film's curtain still takes 12.7 s.
W: The first quarter-minute is the whole judgment for a jury member and for a buyer on a phone. The curtain itself carries no picture, no mark and no sense of progress beyond a hairline most people will not notice, so it reads as a site that has not loaded.
X: Something of the site within two seconds: a still of the house under the curtain, or the page's words first with the picture arriving behind them. Text pages (legal, FAQ, sitemap, 404) should not wait on a 3D scene at all.
N: The wait is the download: the two models are 15.1 MB (estate) and 25.9 MB (hall). Lighter models or a first still are engineering choices, not design changes.

@@ aa-none | SYSTEMIC | systemic | cover-pediment-seam, wall-dashed, glass-rail-dotted, nameplate-aliased

E: Every thin edge in the 3D picture: balusters, cornices, glazing bars, the compound wall's top, plaque edges.
O: The picture is drawn with no edge smoothing. Thin features break into stair-steps and dotted lines, and they crawl as the camera moves.
V: Cover zoom: every baluster on the roof and terrace is a column of square steps. Revolve zoom: the cornice and the compound wall's top edge are dashed white lines. The post-processing chain is set to zero multisampling (PostFX.tsx line 177), so the canvas's own anti-aliasing never reaches the final frame.
W: Jagged, shimmering edges are the first thing that separates a real-time scene from a rendered film, and they sit on the house's most repeated detail. On the client's own 1920×1080 monitor it is at its worst; phones at 2× and 3× hide most of it.
X: Smoothed edges at 1× screens (multisampling on the composer or a final SMAA pass), checked on the integrated graphics card for cost.
